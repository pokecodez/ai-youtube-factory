**Narration Script:**
In December 1900, a ship passing Scotland’s remote Flannan Isles noted the lighthouse beacon was dark. When a relief ship arrived at Eilean Mor days later, they found a chilling scene. The heavy iron station gate was shut. Inside, the clocks had stopped. Two oilskin coats were missing, but the third hung behind the door. A meal sat untouched on the table, and the logbook detailed terrifying gales that—meteorological records later proved—never actually happened. The three experienced keepers—James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur—had vanished completely, leaving behind no bodies, no notes, and no footprints in the mud. Despite an exhaustive search, their fate remains one of maritime history's most enduring enigmas.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts:**
* In December 1900, the lighthouse on Eilean Mor in the Flannan Isles, Scotland, was found unstaffed.
* The three keepers stationed there (Ducat, Marshall, and MacArthur) were missing, and the station was locked from the inside.
* The last log entries reported severe, terrifying storms.
* An investigation confirmed no boats could have safely landed during that period without being noticed. 

**Speculation:**
* The cause of the disappearance (popular theories include a freak rogue wave sweeping them off the cliff while securing equipment, an accidental fall, or internal foul play—though no physical evidence supports violence).
* The discrepancy regarding the weather (meteorological data showed no storms occurred in that immediate area on the dates logged, though local microclimates or psychological stress of the keepers could explain the anomalous entries).
