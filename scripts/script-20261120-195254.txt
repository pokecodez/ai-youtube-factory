**Narration (142 words):**
In November 1930, a veteran trapper named Joe Labelle trudged into a familiar Inuit village near Lake Anjikuni in the remote Canadian wilderness. He expected a warm welcome. Instead, he found an eerie, suffocating silence. 

The village was completely deserted, yet dinner pots still hung over cold, burned-out fires. Heavy winter clothing, rifles, and vital supplies had been left behind in the unlocked cabins. Stranger still, the village’s sled dogs were found starved to death, tied to stakes beneath seven feet of snow. An exhaustive Royal Canadian Mounted Police search turned up no footprints leading away from the settlement. 

To this day, official reports confirm the inexplicable abandonment of the village. But speculation quickly spiraled into local folklore, blaming the bizarre disappearance on harsh elements, mass panic, or even UFOs. What truly happened on the shores of Lake Anjikuni remains unsolved.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts:**
*   In November 1930, a fur trapper named Joe Labelle visited an Inuit settlement near Lake Anjikuni in Nunavut, Canada, and reported it deserted.
*   Labelle stated that he found fires had recently gone out, and that food, supplies, and weapons were left inside the dwellings.
*   The Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP) investigated the incident, noting the presence of dead sled dogs and a lack of outgoing footprints in the heavy snow.

**Speculation / Urban Legend:**
*   Popular retellings (originating decades later in tabloid newspapers and books) claim that an entire village of over 30 inhabitants vanished overnight, and that local graves had been mysteriously dug up and emptied. 
*   Modern paranormal theories suggest alien abduction or supernatural forces, for which there is zero historical or police evidence.
