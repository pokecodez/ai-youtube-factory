**Title:** The Vanishing of the Dover Strait Phantom

**Narration:**
In November 1848, the brilliant French physicist François Arago boarded the steamship *Phocée*, bound for Marseille. As the vessel crossed the Mediterranean, passengers and crew watched in absolute astonishment as a towering, translucent silhouette of Arago himself appeared standing on the open sea, pacing the waves alongside the ship. 

**Visual Suggestion 2:**

**Narration:**
Though documented by multiple credible witnesses, science offers a chilling explanation. This was likely a rare atmospheric phenomenon known as a Fata Morgana—a complex superior mirage where light rays bend through differing temperature layers, projecting distant objects or people onto the horizon. Yet, to this day, the ghostly maritime doppelgänger remains one of history’s most striking optical illusions.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

*   **Verified Facts:**
    *   In November 1848, French physicist François Arago (along with other passengers and crew) reportedly witnessed a phenomenon at sea where a tall, translucent figure resembling Arago appeared to walk alongside the steamship *Phocée*. 
    *   The event was documented and discussed in contemporary scientific circles as an extraordinary visual anomaly.
    *   A "Fata Morgana" is a real meteorological phenomenon (a superior mirage) caused by light bending through thermal layers in the atmosphere, capable of projecting distorted images of distant objects or people onto the horizon or sky.

*   柏 **Speculation:**
    *   Whether the apparition was genuinely a projection of Arago himself, or simply a misidentified mirage of a distant shoreline feature or another passenger magnified by atmospheric conditions, cannot be definitively proven due to the lack of modern scientific instruments at the time. 
    *   The use of terms like "phantom" and "ghostly" are narrative framing devices for the documentary style, not paranormal claims.
