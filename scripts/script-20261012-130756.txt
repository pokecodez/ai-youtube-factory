**Narration:**
In December 1900, a ship passing the remote Flannan Isles off the coast of Scotland noticed something alarming: the island's lighthouse was dark. When relief keeper Joseph Moore finally arrived to investigate, he stepped into a scene of utter eerie abandonment. The entrance gate was shut, the beds were unmade, and the kitchen clock had mysteriously stopped. Most unsettling of all, the heaviest gear—massive ropes and a supply crate—had vanished from a crane landing seventy feet above the churning sea. Despite a thorough investigation, no trace of the three lighthouse keepers was ever found, leaving behind a chilling maritime riddle that remains unsolved to this day.

### **Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown**

*   **Verified Facts:** 
    *   In December 1900, the lighthouse on Eilean Mor (one of the Flannan Isles) was found unlit.
    *   Relief keeper Joseph Moore found the station deserted. The entry gate was closed, beds were unmade, and the kitchen clock was stopped.
    *   Log entries detailed severe weather in the days leading up to the disappearance, and heavy equipment (such as a supply crate and ropes) was reported missing or displaced near the landing stage.
    *   The three keepers (James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur) were never found.

*   **Speculation / Unproven Theories:**
    *   The exact cause of their disappearance. Popular theories range from a sudden, rogue wave washing the men away while they secured equipment, to a tragic accident, or dramatic folktales involving ghosts and sea monsters. (Historical investigations leaned heavily toward a rogue wave, but no conclusive proof exists).
