**Title:** The Vanishing of Lighthouse Island

**Visual Suggestion 1:** (0:00 - 0:15)

**Narration:**
In December 1900, a ship passing the remote Flannan Isles off the coast of Scotland noticed something alarming: the island's lighthouse was unlit. When a relief vessel finally arrived days later, they found a chilling scene. The heavy iron entrance gate was locked, but inside, the station was completely deserted. The kitchen table held untouched meals, a set of oilskins was left behind, and two of the three heavy waterproof coats were missing—meaning someone went out into a raging storm without proper gear. The station’s logbook recorded gale-force winds that no meteorological records for the area actually supported. The three experienced keepers—James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur—vanished without a trace, and no bodies were ever recovered. What drove three seasoned men away from shelter into the freezing Atlantic?

**Verified Facts:**
*   On December 15, 1900, the steamer *Archtor* reported that the Flannan Isles lighthouse was unlit.
*   The relief ship *Hesperus* arrived on December 26, 1900, and found the station abandoned.
*   The entrance gate and station doors were locked, but inside, a meal was left half-eaten, and two of the three keepers' waterproof coats were missing.
*   A thorough search yielded no trace of the three men (Ducat, Marshall, and MacArthur), and their bodies were never found.

**Speculation:**
*   Popular theories suggest the men were swept away by a rogue wave while securing equipment near the landing stage.
*   More sensational, unproven folklore includes tales of madness, foreign spies, or abduction by phantom ships.
