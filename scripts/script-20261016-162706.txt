**Narration (0:00 - 0:50):**
In 1587, over one hundred English settlers arrived on Roanoke Island, off the coast of North Carolina, hoping to establish the first permanent English settlement in the New World. Three years later, when supply ships finally returned, the island was entirely abandoned. No bodies were found, there were no signs of a struggle, and every single building had been carefully dismantled—suggesting the colonists did not leave in a panic. The only clue left behind was a single word carved into a wooden post: "Croatoan." 

To this day, historians debate their fate. Did they seek refuge with the local Croatoan Native American tribe, or attempt to sail back to England on a makeshift boat and perish at sea? Despite centuries of archaeological digs, advanced soil testing, and DNA research, no definitive evidence has ever been recovered to solve the disappearance of the Roanoke colonists.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts:**
*   In 1587, an English expedition led by John White established a settlement of over 100 men, women, and children on Roanoke Island.
*   Governor John White returned to England for supplies shortly after the settlement's founding, but his return was delayed for three years due to the Anglo-Spanish War.
*   When White returned to the island in August 1590, the settlement was deserted, the houses were dismantled, and no defensive fortifications or signs of violence were present.
*   The word "CROATOAN" was carved into a post of the fort, and the letters "CRO" were carved into a nearby tree.
*   Extensive modern archaeological efforts have recovered artifacts, but no conclusive physical evidence has proven the ultimate fate of the colonists.

**Speculation:**
*   The exact fate of the colonists remains unknown. 
*   Popular theories include assimilation into local Native American tribes (such as the Croatoan or Hatteras peoples), relocation to the mainland, or an attempt to build a boat to return to England, resulting in a shipwreck. None of these theories have been definitively proven by verifiable primary evidence.
