**Title:** The Vanishing of Lighthouse Island

**Visual Suggestion 1:** 
*A sweeping drone shot of a desolate, fog-shrouded rocky cliff against a grey sea, transitioning to vintage black-and-white photographs of three Edwardian men.*

**Narration:**
In December 1900, a supply ship reached the remote Flannan Isles lighthouse off the coast of Scotland, only to find something impossible. The station was completely deserted. Inside, the clock had stopped, the fire was unlit, and two of the three waterproof coats were missing—though the gale outside meant nobody would step outside without one. 

**Visual Suggestion 2:** 
*Close-up panning shots of an open logbook under a dim, flickering lamp, showing handwritten entries detailing the howling winds before the sudden stop.*

**Narration:**
The final logbook entries detailed terrifying, howling gales, yet local weather stations recorded no storms in the area that week. Extensive searches yielded no bodies, no wreckage, and no clues. 

**Visual Suggestion 3:** 
*Slow-motion footage of crashing dark waves against jagged rocks, fading into a glowing question mark over the silhouette of a lighthouse.*

### **Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown**

*   **Verified Facts:**
    *   In December 1900, the relief ship *Hesperus* arrived at the Flannan Isles lighthouse and found it unmanned.
    *   The station’s logbook recorded unusual, fearful mentions of severe gales prior to the disappearance, though mainland records showed calm weather.
    *   Two of the keepers' oilskins (coats) were missing, while one was left behind.
    *   A thorough official investigation by the Northern Lighthouse Board concluded that the men were likely swept away by a sudden, freak wave while securing equipment.

*   **Speculation:**
    *   Popular folklore and sensationalist media have attributed the disappearance to sea monsters, foreign abductors, ghost ships, or madness induced by isolation. 
    *   The idea that the men were sitting down to an untouched meal has been widely debunked as a myth added by later storytellers; the official report noted the kitchen was tidy, but not bizarrely abandoned mid-meal.
