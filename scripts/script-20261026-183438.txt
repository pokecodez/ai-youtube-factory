**Title:** The Vanishing Village of Lake Anjikuni: Fact vs. Legend

**Visual Suggestion 1:** 
*Opening shot of a desolate, snow-covered Canadian wilderness transitioning into a vintage, sepia-toned photograph of a frozen lake shoreline.*

**Narration:**
In November 1930, fur trapper Joe Labelle sought shelter at a remote Inuit village by Canada’s Lake Anjikuni. He found a ghost town. 

Fires still smoldered in hearths. Pots of stew remained half-cooked. Abandoned rifles leaned against cabin walls, essential tools left behind in a sub-arctic winter. Most chillingly, a local graveyard had been excavated, its coffins empty. All two hundred villagers had vanished without a trace, leaving behind their sled dogs, which had starved to death tethered to posts.

**Fact vs. Speculation:**
*   **Verified Fact:** The Royal Canadian Mounted Police investigated the report, but official police archives contain no record of an entire village disappearing, and subsequent aerial searches found no evidence of such a settlement.
*   **Speculation:** The sensationalized story was largely fabricated and popularized by writer Frank Edwards in 1959, exaggerating Joe Labelle’s original, less dramatic account of an abandoned campsite into a sci-fi legend of mass disappearance. 

**Narration:**
The true mystery isn't how a village vanished... but how a ghost story was born.
