**Narration:**
In December 1900, a relief ship arrived at the Flannan Isles lighthouse off the coast of Scotland, expecting a bustling station. Instead, they found an eerie silence. The three lighthouse keepers had completely vanished. 

Inside, the fire was cold, meals sat half-eaten, and two of the three heavy waterproof coats were left behind—despite freezing, howling gales outside. The logbook recorded terrifying hurricane-force winds that no other nearby ship had actually reported. The massive iron crane on the landing platform was severely damaged, and safety equipment was missing.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts:**
*   In December 1900, the relief ship *Hesperus* arrived at the Flannan Isles lighthouse and found the station abandoned.
*   The three keepers (James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur) were never seen again.
*   The station’s logbook contained unusual entries regarding severe weather (hurricane-force winds), though mainland weather stations recorded no such storms at that exact time.
*   Equipment, including a heavy iron crane and supply boxes, showed signs of significant storm damage near the landing platform.
*   Inside the living quarters, some foul weather gear was left behind while other coats were taken.

**Speculation:**
*   The idea that a rogue wave swept them away, while considered the most plausible official theory by investigators, remains unproven because no bodies or eyewitnesses were ever found.
*   Popular historical rumors and sensationalized stories of the era—ranging from foreign abduction and ghost ships to a murder-suicide pact among the keepers—lack any physical evidence or documentation.
