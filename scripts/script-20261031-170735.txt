**Narration:**
In December 1900, a ship passing the remote Flannan Isles off the coast of Scotland reported a troubling sight: the lighthouse beacon was unlit. When relief keeper Joseph Moore arrived days later, he stepped into a chilling scene. The station was entirely deserted, yet entirely intact. 

Inside, the kitchen table was set with a meal. The clock had stopped. Two of the keepers’ heavy waterproof coats were missing, but the third was left behind—suggesting one man rushed out unprepared. The last log entries spoke of hurricane-force winds and a weeping keeper, yet weather records showed complete calm. Despite extensive searches, the three men were never seen again.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts (The Historical Record):**
*   In December 1900, the Eilean Mor lighthouse on the Flannan Isles was found unlit by relief keeper Joseph Moore.
*   The three keepers (James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur) were missing.
*   The station was locked from the inside, a meal was left untouched, and two of the three waterproof coats were missing.
*   The final log entries, dated December 12 and 13, mentioned severe storms and distress from one of the keepers, but no severe weather was officially recorded in the area at that exact time.
*   A subsequent official investigation by Robert Muirhead concluded the men likely went down to the western landing stage to secure equipment and were swept away by a rogue wave.

**Speculation (Unsubstantiated Theories):**
*   Popular folklore and sensationalist journalism at the time suggested supernatural causes, alien abduction, foreign spies, or that the men had murdered each other and fled. 
*   Speculation that the log entries described a storm that never happened (though shifting micro-climates in the region are common).
