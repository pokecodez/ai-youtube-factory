In December 1900, a supply ship arrived at the remote Flannan Isles lighthouse off the coast of Scotland. What the crew found inside remains one of maritime history’s most chilling enigmas. The station was completely deserted. 

The dining table still held untouched food. Two of the three heavy waterproof coats were missing, yet the island’s logbook recorded a terrifying gale days earlier—weather that local meteorological records later proved never happened. The lighthouse door was unlocked, and the clocks had stopped. 

How did three experienced keepers vanish without a trace from a locked, isolated rock in the middle of the Atlantic? No bodies were ever recovered. While some blamed rogue waves or freak accidents, others whispered of madness, sea monsters, or phantom storms. To this day, the fate of the Flannan Isles keepers remains unsolved.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts:**
* In December 1900, the supply vessel *Hesperus* arrived at the Flannan Isles lighthouse and found it completely unmanned.
* The station's logbook contained entries detailing severe storm conditions in the days prior to the discovery.
* The lighthouse door was found unlocked, and some foul-weather gear was left behind while others were missing.
* No trace of the three keepers (James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur) was ever found.

**Speculation:**
* The exact cause of the men's disappearance (whether they were swept away by a freak wave while securing equipment near the landing, or fell victim to an accident).
* The discrepancy regarding the weather—whether the logbook entries were misinterpreted, or if an undocumented microburst/rogue wave hit the cliffs. 
* Popular myths and folklore suggesting paranormal causes, ghostly intervention, or psychological distress/madness among the keepers.
