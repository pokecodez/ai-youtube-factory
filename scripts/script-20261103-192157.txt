**Title:** The Vanishing Village of Lake Anjikuni: Fact vs. Legend

**Visual Suggestion 1:** A slow, atmospheric drone shot over a desolate, snowy Canadian wilderness, transitioning to grainy, sepia-toned archival photos of Inuit people from the 1930s.

**Narration Script:**
In November 1930, fur trapper Joe Labelle arrived at a routine Inuit village near Lake Anjikuni in Northern Canada. He found something chilling: the settlement was completely deserted, yet pots of caribou stew still simmered over dying embers, and loaded rifles leaned helplessly against cabin walls. Worse, the villagers’ sled dogs were found starved to death, chained outside homes packed with food. 

Did an entire community vanish into thin air? 

Here are the verified facts: The Royal Canadian Mounted Police investigated and found no signs of struggle, and heavy snow made footprints impossible to track. However, the sensational claim that grave sites were emptied and glowing lights were seen in the sky? That was entirely fabricated by newspapers years later. 

So, what happened? The true mystery remains unsolved.
