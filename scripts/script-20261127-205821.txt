**Narration (138 words):**
In November 1930, Canadian fur trapper Joe Labelle sought shelter at an Inuit village near Lake Anjikuni. He found the settlement completely deserted. Fires still smoldered beneath half-cooked meals, and doors stood unlocked. Most chillingly, several graves in the local cemetery appeared dug up and empty. 

The Royal Canadian Mounted Police launched an investigation, confirming the village was abandoned and supplies were left behind. However, no bodies were ever recovered. 

Sensationalist newspapers soon blamed UFOs and mass abductions, turning a remote tragedy into global folklore. But what does the verified record show? Subsequent police reports revealed no evidence of a supernatural event, nor has official documentation ever corroborated Labelle’s most extreme claims regarding the graves. Today, historians suggest the villagers simply dispersed during a harsh winter—leaving behind a lingering ghost story built on exaggeration.
