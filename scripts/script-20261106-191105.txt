**Narration:**
In December 1900, a routine supply ship arrived at the Flannan Isles lighthouse off the coast of Scotland. What the crew found inside would baffle investigators for over a century. The three experienced keepers assigned to the remote station were completely gone. The heavy entrance door was bolted from the inside. Inside, a meal remained half-eaten, and two of the heavy waterproof coats were missing—yet the third man’s coat was left behind, despite the freezing December gale outside. The station’s logbook contained frantic, erratic final entries, detailing howling winds and a weeping keeper. Despite exhaustive searches, no trace of the men was ever found.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts:**
*   In December 1900, the relief ship *Hesperus* arrived at the Flannan Isles lighthouse and found the station abandoned.
*   The three keepers—James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur—were missing, and no bodies were ever recovered.
*   The main gate was secured, a meal was left uneaten, and two of the three men's waterproof jackets were missing.
*   The station’s logbook contained unusual weather reports and entries leading up to the disappearance.

**Speculation:**
*   Popular theories include the men being swept away by a rogue, freak wave while attempting to secure equipment near the water's edge, or an accident involving treacherous cliffs. 
*   Sensationalized fringe theories (such as foreign
 abduction, phantom ships, or sea monsters) have been popularized in fiction and folklore, but are unsupported by any physical evidence.
