**Narration:**
In December 1900, a supply ship arrived at the remote Eilean Mor lighthouse in Scotland, only to find it completely abandoned. The heavy iron entrance gate was locked. Inside, a half-eaten meal sat on the table, and the clock had mysteriously stopped. Most chillingly, the station’s logbook recorded gale-force winds—yet local meteorological stations confirmed the weather had been entirely calm. The three experienced keepers vanished without a trace, leaving behind oilskins and boots. No bodies were ever recovered. What drove seasoned men away from safety into the freezing Atlantic? Did a sudden, freak rogue wave sweep them off the rocks? Or did something far more inexplicable happen in the dark? To this day, the fate of the Eilean Mor keepers remains one of maritime history’s most baffling and enduring enigmas.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts:**
*   In December 1900, the supply steamer *Hesperus* arrived at the Eilean Mor lighthouse in the Outer Hebrides and found the station unmanned.
*   The three keepers (James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur) were missing. 
*   The main gate was locked, the kitchen table had a meal prepared, and the station's logbook contained entries up to December 12.
*   A set of oilskins was left behind, indicating at least one man went outside without waterproof gear.
*   No bodies or traces of the men were ever found.

**Speculation:**
*   The exact cause of the disappearance (e.g., whether they were swept away by an unexpected rogue wave while securing equipment, or if internal conflict/mental breakdown played a role).
*   The discrepancy regarding the weather (while the log noted storms, mainland records indicated calm weather, though localized severe sea swells in that region are notoriously unpredictable).
