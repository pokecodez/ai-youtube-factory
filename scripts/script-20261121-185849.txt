**Narration:**
In December 1900, a supply ship arrived at the remote Flannan Isles lighthouse in Scotland, only to find something impossible. The island was completely deserted. Inside the station, the clocks had stopped. The kitchen fire was cold, but untouched meals sat nearby. Worst of all, the heavy iron entrance gate was securely latched, and two of the keepers' oilskins were missing—meaning someone had gone out into the freezing storm without proper gear. Yet, the official logbook detailed terrifying winds that had battered the island days prior. A search yielded zero traces of the three men. No bodies were ever found. To this day, the fate of the keepers remains officially unexplained, leaving behind a chilling maritime riddle at the edge of the world.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts:**
*   In December 1900, the supply vessel *Hesperus* arrived at the Flannan Isles lighthouse and found the station abandoned.
*   The three keepers (James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur) were missing, and no trace of them was ever found.
*   The station's logbook contained entries describing severe, unusual storms in the days leading up to their disappearance.
*   The lighthouse gate was bolted shut, and two of the men's waterproof coats were missing.

**Speculation:**
*   The exact cause of the men's disappearance (e.g., whether they were swept away by a rogue wave while securing equipment, fell victim to an accident, or experienced some other unforeseen tragedy) remains unknown due to a lack of eyewitnesses or physical evidence. (Popular folklore at the time suggested supernatural causes, but historians and maritime experts attribute the event to natural environmental hazards).
