**Title:** The Vanishing of Lighthouse Island

**Visual Suggestion 1:** 
*A moody, slow-panning drone shot over crashing, grey waves hitting a jagged, isolated Scottish cliffside under a heavy, overcast sky.*

**Narration:**
In December 1900, a supply ship arrived at the remote Flannan Isles lighthouse off the coast of Scotland. It found a chilling scene. The heavy entrance gate was shut, the kitchen fire was unlit, and the clocks had stopped. Inside, all three lighthouse keepers were completely gone. The island’s logbook recorded severe storms days prior, yet weather logs from the mainland showed no such gales. Search parties scoured the precipitous cliffs, but no bodies were ever recovered. What forces could make three experienced men abandon their post without their oilskins in the dead of winter? While some whisper of sea monsters, ghosts, or foreign abduction, oceanographers suggest a rogue wave may have swept the men away while securing equipment near the cliffs. To this day, the exact fate of the Flannan Isles keepers remains unsolved.

### Fact vs. Speculation Breakdown

**Verified Facts:**
*   In December 1900, the supply vessel *Hesperus* arrived at the Flannan Isles lighthouse and found it abandoned.
*   The three keepers (James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald MacArthur) were missing.
*   The lighthouse station was unoccupied, clocks were stopped, and a meal was left half-eaten/unprepared.
*   No bodies or trace of the men were ever found despite extensive searches.

**Speculation:**
*   The idea that the men were taken by sea monsters, ghosts, or foreign agents (popularized in folklore and fiction, such as the poem *Flannan Isle* by Wilfrid Wilson Gibson).
*   The exact mechanics of how they disappeared (the rogue wave theory is a plausible hypothesis proposed by investigators and researchers, but it cannot be definitively proven without eyewitnesses or physical evidence).
